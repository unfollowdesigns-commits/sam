January here is fifteen degrees and people are in coats anyway. The
temperature is not the point. The point is that the sun never gets very far
up, so it comes into rooms sideways for most of the day, and a curtain will
hold a rectangle of light for an hour instead of ten minutes.

That is the only reason **Winter Light** exists as a separate body of work.
The frames are quiet because there was time to make them quiet.

## What it means for the film

Low sun is generous and then it is suddenly gone. Around four the light
drops fast enough that a roll started at noon finishes at a completely
different exposure, and the two halves of the roll do not look like they were
shot on the same day.

- Shoot the interiors first, while the sun is still coming through windows.
- Leave the street until three, when the shadows get long enough to be a
  subject rather than a nuisance.
- After five, either push the film or stop.

None of this is advice so much as a description of what I ended up doing
after getting it wrong for two winters.
